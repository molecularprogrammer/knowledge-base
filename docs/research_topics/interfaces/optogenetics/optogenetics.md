Optogenetics involves genetically encoded, light-sensitive proteins (such as channelrhodopsins or photoreceptors) used to control living cells, tissues, or neural activity in vivo or in cell culture.

Primary Objective	Dynamic, real-time spatial and temporal control of biological processes,
signaling, or gene expression.

Operating Environment	Typically inside living cells, tissues, or whole organisms (in vivo or cell
culture).

Core Mechanism	Light-induced protein-protein dimerization or conformational shifts (e.g., using
plant photoreceptors like CRY2/CIBN or LOV domains).

Role in Molecular Programming	Acts as an actuator or regulatory interface to command cellular machinery using light signals.
