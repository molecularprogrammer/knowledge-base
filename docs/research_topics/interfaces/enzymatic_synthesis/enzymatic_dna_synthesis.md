Enzymatic DNA Synthesis (EDS)—which uses engineered enzymes like Terminal Deoxynucleotidyl
Transferase (TdT) to polymerize DNA without traditional phosphoramidite chemistry—fits primarily
under Interfaces or Systems, depending on whether you emphasize the digital-to-biochemical
translation hardware or the production engine for molecular engineering.

The Hardware-Software Bridge: EDS acts as a foundational translation layer. It takes digital text files (sequences) and converts them into physical oligonucleotides or genes.

Bio-electronic/Biochemical Interfacing: Because it relies on enzymatic control (such as transiently
capping nucleotides or using electrochemical/photocontrol to trigger enzymatic addition), it
represents a cutting-edge biochemical interface between digital control systems and molecular
construction.

While laser-driven microarray selection is typically optimized for parallel, in situ high-throughput screening and chip-based library generation, enzymatic DNA synthesis is generally focused on scalable, clean, and potentially longer-read sequence writing using biological catalysts rather than harsh organic solvents. Both occupy the upstream creation space of molecular programming, bridging the gap between digital design and physical molecules.
